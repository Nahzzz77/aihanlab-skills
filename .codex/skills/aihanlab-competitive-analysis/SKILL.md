# AiHanLab Competitive Analysis

## Overview

Produce decision-ready competitive analysis from imperfect evidence. Inspect what the competitor actually enables, separate proof from inference, adapt comparison dimensions to the category, and end with prioritized product choices.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the decision.** Identify the competitor, focus, user product, target user, research date, and decision to inform. Infer low-risk context from supplied material; ask only when a missing choice would materially change the analysis.
2. **Build the evidence set.** Prefer hands-on observation and authoritative primary sources, then credible reporting and attributed user evidence. Record artifact provenance and access limits.
3. **Inspect the real journey.** Reconstruct entry, setup, core task, result, edit or confirm, continued use, collaboration, monetization, and failure or recovery wherever observable.
4. **Grade claims and capabilities.** Read [references/evidence-and-ratings.md](references/evidence-and-ratings.md) before assigning labels or ratings. Keep evidence status separate from capability quality.
5. **Adapt to the category.** Read [references/industry-overlays.md](references/industry-overlays.md) after identifying the category. Use the universal core plus only the relevant overlay. For a new category, derive dimensions from user jobs, switching criteria, and failure costs.
6. **Compare with weights.** Weight dimensions by their importance to the stated decision. Explain each rating, surface low-confidence rows, and include sensitivity when uncertain weights could change the conclusion.
7. **Analyze positioning.** Compare target user, category claim, promised outcome, differentiator, proof, switching logic, and vulnerable claims.
8. **Make product decisions.** State what to match, where to differentiate, what to deprioritize, what to validate next, the main threat, and the competitor's plausible winning scenario.
9. **Deliver the artifact.** Read [references/output-templates.md](references/output-templates.md) and select the smallest format that answers the decision. Lead with the competitor's center of gravity, not a feature inventory.

## Non-negotiable evidence rules

- Use `Verified`, `Indicated`, `Reported`, `Inferred`, and `Not observed` exactly as defined in the evidence reference.
- Use `Strong`, `Adequate`, `Weak`, `Absent`, and `Unverified` only for capability quality.
- Treat a visible button or menu as an entry point, not proof of workflow depth.
- Treat marketing claims as claims until product evidence verifies them.
- Never turn `Not observed` into `Absent` without sufficient evidence.
- Date time-sensitive findings and identify material assumptions.

## Safety and research boundaries

- Analyze only public or user-authorized material.
- Do not bypass login, paywalls, rate limits, anti-bot controls, permissions, or technical protections.
- Do not expose private recordings, local paths, credentials, account details, internal roadmaps, or confidential product plans in public artifacts.
- Summarize protected material; do not republish substantial copyrighted text, images, recordings, or datasets.
- For reviews and social posts, retain provenance, date, sample limitations, and possible commercial bias. Attribute warnings unless independently verified facts support them.
- When public social signals affect personalization, examine platform terms, consent, data minimization, sensitive-trait inference, location safety, correction, deletion, and opt-out controls.

## Quick quality gate

Before finalizing, confirm that every material conclusion has an evidence label, every rating has a reason, industry dimensions match the user's decision, unknowns remain unknown, and recommendations are prioritized rather than exhaustive.
